# Holded, Odoo or software built for you: how to choose without getting it wrong

<div className="mt-6 border-l-2 border-brand pl-5 text-[18px] leading-relaxed text-fg">
  You will find twenty comparisons and all of them say something different. This is not the
  twenty-first: it is the question none of them asks, which is not which of the two is better but
  how much of your work fits inside a catalogue.
</div>

## Why is no comparison you will read neutral?

Search for "Odoo vs Holded" and count: nearly every result is signed by a consultancy that
implements Odoo or a reseller who sells Holded. You do not have to assume bad faith. Someone who
has spent six years implementing Odoo has watched Odoo solve problems that a Holded reseller has
watched Holded solve, and each writes from what they have seen work.

The problem is not that they lie: it is that **neither of them can write the honest conclusion**,
which is often "what you already have is fine" or "neither of these solves this". We are not
neutral either — we build custom software — so we say so up front, and further down we spell out
the signal that what we sell is **not** what you need.

## What fits in an off-the-shelf system, and what does not?

There is a test that takes five minutes and is right nearly every time. **Look at how you work out
a price.**

If one of your quotes comes from quantity times price, a discount and VAT on top, you fit almost
any product on the market and there is nothing more to discuss: buy the one you like best and
spend the money on something else.

If it comes from a rule that mixes the area, the type of installation, the special rate you have
given that client for nine years, how long it took the last time you went, or the surcharge for
working on a roof, then it does not fit. And here is the part that matters: **it does not fit in a
way that configuration will not fix**. It gets fixed by someone filling the gap by hand every
month.

## What should never be built from scratch?

This is the part almost nobody says while selling custom development, and it is the part that
saves the most money.

Accounting, tax, bank reconciliation, the returns you file and compliance with VeriFactu and
mandatory e-invoicing **are exactly the same in every company in Spain**. They do not set you
apart from anyone, they will not win you a client, and keeping them current is endless work: every
time a rule changes, they have to be touched again.

You buy that ready-made. Always.

## So what does get built?

What makes you different from the firm next door. Which is almost always daily operations and
almost never the accounts:

- **The pricing rules that are yours**, which no catalogue ships, because you invented them over
  years.
- **The job report** in the shape your client needs, which is not the shape the neighbour's client
  needs.
- **The path of an incoming request**: who takes it, how it is prioritised, who it goes to and how
  it ends up as a job and an invoice.
- **The hours**, when people work away from the office and clocking in has to work where there is
  no signal.

And then it is connected to the rest, so a figure is typed once and reaches both places. That is
the answer almost no comparison gives, because **it is not "one of the two": it is both**, each
doing what it does well.

## How do you know you are overbuilding?

The mirror-image trap, and the one it falls to us to flag.

If what you want to build already exists and your version would only be "similar but ours", do not
build it. If the reason for going custom is that you "don't like" the current system and there is
no specific process behind it that does not fit, likewise. And if the company is three people and
the real problem is that nobody writes down what they do, a new system will not fix that: it will
amplify it, which is what <Link href="/blog/por-que-diagnosticar-antes-de-automatizar">the article
on diagnosing before automating</Link> is about.

## Which cost never appears on the price list?

The one that decides the sum after two or three years. A licence is easy to compare because it is
two numbers; what does not get compared is time.

If someone spends four hours a month on the parallel spreadsheet, the report that gets rebuilt and
typing the same figure twice, that is forty-eight hours a year of a person who draws a salary.
Multiply it by the years you have had the workaround. That number appears in no comparison
**because the company selling the software does not pay it**, you do, in wages, which is why it
never makes the table.

The honest sum is not "licence versus development". It is "licence plus workaround hours" versus
"licence plus development, without the workaround hours".

## What if you already run one of them?

You almost never have to throw it out, and be wary of anyone who says otherwise before looking at
how you work.

Typically the system you have handles the accounting and tax side well — the side you should not
touch — and falls short out on the road: job reports, hours, incoming requests and quotes with
awkward rules. That can be built alongside and connected. Migrating wholesale is the expensive
option, it is the one most often sold, and it is the one least often needed.
