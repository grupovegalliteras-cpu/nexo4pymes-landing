# Job reports on a phone: why the ones that fail, fail

<div className="mt-6 border-l-2 border-brand pl-5 text-[18px] leading-relaxed text-fg">
  Moving job reports from paper to a phone looks like the easiest change of all, and it is one of
  the most often abandoned after three months. Not because of the technology: because the report
  gets designed around what the office wants to know, and it is filled in by somebody standing on a
  rooftop at two in the afternoon.
</div>

## What do paper job reports cost you?

Paper does not fail because it is paper. It fails because of the journey it makes afterwards.

Added up, that is a company invoicing a week late and losing money on materials it really did use.
It is not a tidiness problem: it is cash.

## Why do job report apps get abandoned?

Here is the uncomfortable part, and we have seen it several times in companies that had already
tried before calling us.

The digital report gets designed in an office meeting. And in an office meeting fields always get
added, because each one looks free: while they are filling it in, let them add the serial number
too, and the vehicle plate, and a general condition rating. Thirty fields later, filling in the
report takes longer than writing it by hand.

<Destacado titulo="The test worth running before buying anything">
  Time how long it takes a technician to close a report on a phone, standing up, wearing gloves, and
  compare it with writing it on paper.

  If the phone does not win, the app will be abandoned. It does not matter how good the reports look
  in the office: **the person who decides whether this works is the person filling it in**, and they
  decide in the first week.
</Destacado>

## What does a job report need to include?

The short list, the one genuinely needed to invoice and to defend yourself:

- **Who, when and where.** Comes from clocking in and from the job itself; nobody types it.
- **What was done.** Free text, short, plus ticked tasks if it is routine maintenance.
- **How long it took.** If clocking in and the report are the same thing, this comes for free too.
- **Materials used.** From a list of what is usual in your trade, not by typing full names.
- **Photos.** Two: before and after. Worth more than three paragraphs.
- **The customer's signature.** On screen, at the time, with the name and time stored beside it.

Everything else is optional and should stay that way. A field that is not used to invoice or to
decide anything is a field whose only effect is making the report take longer.

<Nota>
  In contract maintenance every customer tends to want their own format. The answer is different
  report templates, not one generic report somebody rewrites back at the office. The technician sees
  that customer's fields and no others.
</Nota>

## Does it work without a signal?

Half of Mallorca has poor coverage: hotel basements, plant rooms, rooftops, inland estates. A
report that needs a connection to be closed ends up being filled in from the van on the way out, or
at home at night, which is exactly the situation you were trying to avoid.

The report is filled in on the phone with no network and syncs when coverage returns. It is a
requirement, not an advanced feature: without it, the rest does not matter.

## What changes in the office?

When the report is closed on site, the office stops chasing information.

It knows what was done the same day, it can invoice without calling anyone, and when a customer
disputes a job there is a signature, a time and photos. The hours on the report go to payroll and
the materials go to the <Link href="/modulos/facturacion-verifactu">invoice</Link> without anybody
typing them again.

And there is an effect nobody mentions: technicians stop getting calls from the office asking about
jobs from three days ago. That is a good half hour a day nobody was counting as a cost.
