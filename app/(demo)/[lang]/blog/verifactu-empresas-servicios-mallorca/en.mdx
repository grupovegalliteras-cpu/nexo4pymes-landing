# VeriFactu: what a service company in Mallorca actually has to do

<div className="mt-6 border-l-2 border-brand pl-5 text-[18px] leading-relaxed text-fg">
  The VeriFactu deadline has moved three times, and that has done more damage than the rule itself:
  plenty of people have stopped paying attention. Here is what binds today, what has only been
  announced, and the four things worth doing now even if the date moves again.
</div>

## What is binding today, and what is only an announcement?

Two things get mixed up constantly, in conversation and in the odd headline.

**What is published and binds.** The invoicing systems regulation (Royal Decree 1007/2023) has
applied to software makers since 29 July 2025: the programs they sell have to comply. For those
who issue invoices, Royal Decree-Law 15/2025 set **1 January 2027** for companies paying corporate
income tax and **1 July 2027** for the self-employed and other taxpayers.

**What has been announced and does not bind yet.** On 5 October 2026 the Spanish Ministry of
Finance announced moving the obligation to October 2028 for anyone invoicing under eight million
euros. That postponement has not appeared in the official gazette.

<Destacado titulo="Why this distinction is not legal nit-picking">
  An announcement that never gets published can come to nothing, can come back worded differently,
  or can come late. Until it is in the gazette, **the date that binds you is still the 2027 one**.

  And even if it is published exactly as announced, you would gain eighteen months to do the same
  work. It does not change what has to be done: it changes when it catches up with you.
</Destacado>

## What actually changes in your invoices?

This is the part almost nobody explains, and the only one worth understanding. VeriFactu is not
about sending a file to the tax agency once a year. It is about how your invoicing software
behaves.

At the moment each invoice is issued, the software has to produce an **invoicing record**: an entry
with that invoice's essential data and a **digital fingerprint** calculated from them and from the
previous record's fingerprint. Every record is hooked to the one before it, like links in a chain.

That chain is the whole idea. If somebody deletes an invoice or changes an amount, the next link
stops fitting and it shows. That is why the regulation forbids software from allowing anything to
be deleted or altered without a trace: a cancelled invoice is cancelled with another entry, not by
disappearing.

The invoice also carries a **QR code** and, if you are in the mode that submits records, a note
saying it can be verified on the tax agency's site.

## Which of the two modes suits a small company?

The regulation lets you choose between two ways of complying:

1. **VERI*FACTU**: every invoicing record is sent to the tax agency as the invoice is issued. In
   exchange, you do not have to sign the records electronically or keep anything special: the
   agency has them.
2. **Non-verifiable system**: the records stay in your system, electronically signed and chained,
   available to the agency on request. You have to keep them and answer for them.

<Nota>
  For a service company of ten or fifteen people, the first is usually less work. The second makes
  sense when there is a real reason not to submit in real time — poor connectivity on site, for
  example — and somebody who takes responsibility for keeping the records.
</Nota>

## The part that really hurts: typing the same invoice twice

This is where the rule stops being paperwork and becomes a decision about how you work.

In most of the maintenance, cleaning, HVAC and pool companies in Mallorca we talk to, the sequence
goes like this: the technician writes hours and materials on a paper job sheet or in a WhatsApp
message, somebody in the office copies it into a spreadsheet, and from there somebody types it
again into the invoicing program. The same data, three times.

If your invoicing program is an island, making it VeriFactu-compliant fixes none of that: it just
adds a digital fingerprint to a process that still has three manual copies. The real opportunity is
the opposite one: the invoice coming out of the <Link href="/modulos/partes-de-trabajo">job report
the technician has already closed and the customer has already signed</Link>, with hours and
materials already on it.

## What should you do now, and in what order?

**First, ask your current provider.** In writing, with two specific questions: will you issue the
declaration of compliance, and on what date will the ready version be available? A clear answer
with a date saves you the rest of this article. Silence, or an "we're working on it" from six
months ago, is the sign to start looking at alternatives.

**Second, look at where your invoices originate.** Not which program: which moment. If they come
out of a job report, a delivery note or a maintenance contract, that is where to start, because
that is where the duplicated work is.

**Third, decide the mode.** It is one configuration setting, but it is worth deciding knowingly
rather than by default.

**Fourth, test it with real invoices before the deadline.** A month before, not the week of. The
change touches numbering, cancellations and credit notes, which is where the surprises live.

<Destacado titulo="What not to do">
  Do not buy in a hurry the month before the deadline. That is exactly when the worst migrations get
  sold, and at the worst prices, because the seller knows you have no time to compare.

  And do not assume your program will adapt **because it is expensive or because it has been around
  for years**. Several makers have announced they will not adapt their older versions: the
  compliance arrives in a new version, charged separately. Ask first.
</Destacado>
