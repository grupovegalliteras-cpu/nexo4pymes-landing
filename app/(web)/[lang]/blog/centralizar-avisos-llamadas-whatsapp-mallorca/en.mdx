# How to organise calls, WhatsApp and customer requests in a Mallorca business

<div className="mt-6 border-l-2 border-coral pl-5 text-[18px] leading-relaxed text-[#F4F6FF]">
  Your customers do not all have to use the same channel. What matters is that everything coming

  feel the difference after the first two.
</div>

## Why small companies lose customer requests

Picture a [maintenance](/sectores/mantenimiento) company with an office and several technicians
spread across the island.

One customer rings about a breakdown. Another sends a photo on WhatsApp. An email with a request
arrives at the office. And a technician reports a problem by phone while on another job.

Everyone sorts out what they can, but the information ends up scattered across phones, chats,
inboxes and notes on paper.

This works while there are few requests. As soon as the volume rises, it gets hard to know what
is outstanding and who has to act.

The problem is not using WhatsApp or the phone: they are good channels and customers prefer
them. The problem is relying on them as the **only** system for controlling the work.

## The four steps to centralise it

### 1. Log every request in one place

Every call, message, email or form should become a record with the basics: customer, reason,
date, priority and status. From then on anyone on the team can check a request without depending
on whoever answered the phone.

### 2. Separate urgent from what can wait

Not all requests weigh the same. Categories and a few simple rules tell a breakdown that has
left a customer without water from a question about prices. Each business sets its own rules:
what counts as urgent for a pool company is not what counts for a renovation firm.

### 3. Give every request an owner and a status

One person responsible and a clear status: pending, assigned, in progress, resolved or awaiting
confirmation. With that, the office knows what needs attention without ringing every technician
to ask.

### 4. Connect it to what comes next

A request turns into a visit, a job report, a quote and an invoice. If the tools are connected,
that data gets reused at each step instead of being typed again. That is exactly what the
[operations and invoicing modules](/modulos) do.

<Nota>
  Not every company needs all four steps. Logging and assigning requests properly — the first
  two — usually removes most of the noise. The rest can come later, when it is clearly needed.
</Nota>

## Which companies it suits

Businesses that receive customer requests and coordinate people, visits or incidents:

- Maintenance of residential blocks and buildings.
- Electrical, [HVAC](/sectores/climatizacion) and plumbing installers.
- [Pool](/sectores/piscinas) companies and technical services.
- Repairs and breakdown assistance.
- [Multi-service firms](/sectores/mantenimiento) with several technicians.
- Property managers tracking incidents across several buildings.

In these businesses the work does not end when the phone rings: someone has to assign it,
coordinate the visit and check it was resolved. That is why centralising requests pays off more
than switching on automatic replies in WhatsApp.

## Hire someone, or automate?

It depends. If the workload consistently exceeds what the team can handle, you need to hire:
technology does not replace dealing with customers or resolving problems.

But before expanding the payroll it is worth measuring how much time goes on logging messages,
hunting for information, returning calls and asking about the status of a job. When a good part
of that time is lost to disorder rather than volume, a system pays off more than a hire.

The rule is the same as always: [find the bottleneck before you
invest](/blog/por-que-diagnosticar-antes-de-automatizar).
